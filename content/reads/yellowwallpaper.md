<u>GoodReads Overview:</u> Diagnosed by her physician husband with a “temporary nervous depression—a slight hysterical tendency” after the birth of her child, a woman is urged to rest for the summer in an old colonial mansion. Forbidden from doing work of any kind, she spends her days in the house’s former nursery, with its barred windows, scratched floor, and peeling yellow wallpaper. In a private journal, the woman records her growing obsession with the “horrid” wallpaper

<u>Mode:</u> Online.

<u>My Thoughts:</u> This could be the shortest book I've ever read, and yet it was intensely creepy and depressing. It has a really poetic sound to it, and the alternating paragraphs of outside world to inside her room, the “yellow wallpaper” room, were very interesting for me to read. That is, if we only talk about the writing style, and not its content.

This book can be interpreted in a variety of ways, many theories on what exactly could be the meaning of her psychosis. The house, where she was meant to rest, could be haunted, or that she was not taken care of properly, or even that she suffered from PPD (postpartum depression), but what would a 1800s book know about PPD?

The fact that her husband John and her brother, both being physicians and males, must mean they are correct at all times, and that anything she felt otherwise was irrelevant, is absolutely jarring all throughout the book. In my opinion, she believes (as she mentions in her journal entries) that she needs a change of routine, or perhaps even a change of environment, but that is disregarded and John being her physician forbids her from doing any work, not even writing. Which in turn affected her health even worse, to the point she starts fixating her whole mind on that yellow wallpaper pattern in her room, decoding it and freeing the woman she thinks is trapped inside it. She feels guilty that she takes her husband’s and her sister’s care for granted, and the unintentional pressure of getting better quickly for the kid, to stop being a burden to everyone, everything compounds enough to break her.

The book ends abruptly, hauntingly, and the cure for her mental health is never discovered. Interesting read to sit and ponder about one of many theories.
