<u>GoodReads Overview:</u> Thrown in prison for a crime he has not committed, Edmond Dantes is confined to the grim fortress of If. There he learns of a great hoard of treasure hidden on the Isle of Monte Cristo and becomes determined not only to escape but to unearth the treasure and use it to plot the destruction of the three men responsible for his incarceration. A huge popular success when it was first serialized in the 1840s, Dumas was inspired by a real-life case of wrongful imprisonment when writing his epic tale of suffering and retribution.

<u>Mode:</u> E-book.

<u>My Thoughts:</u> It took me about 3 months to read this book, but I was reading rather slowly, and i also paired this novel with other short novels, so I have mixed feelings about reading it. The plot was good, there were many, many subplots within subplots that I genuinely enjoyed, but some of them felt a little inconsistent. I did not like the writing style much, sometimes the writing felt shallow like a bit too long without actually delivering anything in particular. But maybe I am judging too hard, this book was an adventure book, a revenge plot, and nothing more. There wasn’t much to ponder about, but to just read it and enjoy. What I did enjoy was the way Dumas plotted the revenge for each character who wronged Edmond Dantès, and some of the plot twists that I couldn’t have guessed right away. The book is also an easy read, it's considered a gateway to start reading classics or reading in general, the story is easy to follow along. I was in for the vibes and I was quite satisfied. There were a lot of characters and also some of the different main characters’ personas, with their own stories, which was fun to read. Though personally, I thought some characters were left without a proper closure (we have no idea what became of Franz), some characters had a satisfying ending (whatever Eugene did was really cool). I rate this book about 3 / 5 stars, for it was purely entertaining to read.
